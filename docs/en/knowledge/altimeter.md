# Transition Altitude & Altimeter Settings (QNH/QFE/QNE)

> Content on this page is compiled from public sources for learning purposes only. Refer to the currently effective AIP and airport information for actual values.

## Three Altimeter Pressure Settings

The altitude shown by the altimeter depends on the pressure setting. The three common settings are:

| Setting | Meaning | Altimeter indication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **QNH** | Local pressure reduced to mean sea level | Altitude above mean sea level (approximately elevation) |
| **QFE** | Station pressure at the aerodrome | Height above the aerodrome elevation |
| **QNE** | Standard pressure (1013.25 hPa / 29.92 inHg) | Pressure altitude; used uniformly at and above the transition level |

## Transition Altitude and Transition Level

- **Transition Altitude (TA)**: when climbing through this altitude within the terminal area, reset the altimeter from QNH to QNE; thereafter, altitudes are expressed as flight levels (FL). In most of mainland China the transition altitude is **3,000 m**, with adjustments at plateau and high-elevation airports — refer to airport information.
- **Transition Level (TL)**: the first usable flight level above the standard pressure surface. In most of mainland China it is **3,600 m** (approximately FL118).
- The band between the transition altitude and the transition level is called the **transition layer**; crossing it is prohibited so that vertical separation is preserved while transitioning from aerodrome pressure to standard pressure.

## Flight Levels

Above the transition level, altitudes are expressed as **flight levels (FL)**: set the altimeter to QNE and take "height in feet ÷ 100", e.g., FL118 means 11,800 ft.

The metric-to-foot conversion is 1 m = 3.28084 ft. Chinese cruise flight levels are filled in metric; for the comparison, see [RVSM and Metric Flight Levels in China](/en/knowledge/rvsm).

## Application to Online Flight

1. During approach and departure, set the altimeter to the airport QNH specified by the controller (or QFE when required).
2. In cruise, set the altimeter to QNE and use flight levels as instructed by the controller.
3. For flights departing from mainland China, the cruise flight level in the flight plan must follow the CAAC RVSM allocation rules (see [User Guidelines](/en/rules/user-rules)).
