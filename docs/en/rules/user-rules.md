# XFLYSIM User Guidelines (Provisional)

> These rules take effect at 00:00 Beijing Time on September 26, 2026.

## Chapter I — General Provisions

**Article 1**　These rules are formulated to standardize the order of the online flight (hereinafter referred to as "online flight") server and to maintain the online flight environment.

**Article 2**　These rules apply to all members who connect to the XFLYSIM online flight server (hereinafter referred to as "the server") at `ol.xflysim.com` for online flight. All members must comply with these rules while conducting online flight, and must also comply with the relevant laws and regulations of the state.

**Article 3**　Members who violate these rules will receive a platform warning. Aircraft pilots who fail to correct the violation after being warned will be handled by the platform administrator in accordance with the Violation Handling Methods in Chapter III of these rules.

**Article 4**　The terms used in these rules are defined as follows:

- **Aircraft Pilot**: a user who uses online flight software to connect to the server and conduct flight.
- **Controlled Airspace**: a specific area where a controller is logged on to the server and provides air traffic control services. The area is determined by the horizontal and vertical limits of the control area published in the currently effective Aeronautical Information Publication (AIP).
- **Activity Airspace**: the sum of the tower control areas, approach (terminal) control areas and all area control areas (including high and low altitude control areas) through which the activity routes pass, for the departure airports, arrival airports and en-route stopover airports published in the online flight website activity thread. The limits of the above control areas are determined by the horizontal and vertical limits published in the currently effective AIP.
- **Activity Period**: the period from one hour before the activity start time published in the online flight website activity thread until all controllers in the activity airspace have closed the corresponding frequency of their control positions.
- **AFK Flight**: a state in which an aircraft pilot is connected to the server but is unable to respond to the instructions of controllers or platform supervisors, including but not limited to going out for meals, sleeping, etc. while connected to the server.

## Chapter II — General Online Flight Rules

### General Rules

**Article 5**　Aircraft pilots shall use a valid callsign registered on the online flight website to connect to the server. The callsign is the unique identifier of the aircraft pilot on the server.

**Article 6**　Aircraft pilots are strictly prohibited from connecting military aircraft, aircraft that have not obtained airworthiness certification, or aircraft with a design cruise speed greater than Mach 1.0 to the server.

**Article 7**　Aircraft pilots shall connect to the server at the fixed parking stands designated by the airport. Connecting directly on taxiways, runways or other non-stand positions is strictly prohibited. In airspace where air traffic control services are provided, if special reasons require connecting at other locations (including temporary stands), the pilot must coordinate with the controller providing air traffic control services at that location and confirm that there is no impact before connecting. If after connecting there is position overlap or an impact on the normal flight of other aircraft pilots, the pilot shall disconnect immediately, move to a position with no impact, or reconnect only after confirming that the impact has been eliminated.

**Article 8**　After connecting to the server, aircraft pilots shall submit a valid flight plan as soon as possible. The flight plan must contain the following information and must be filled in correctly:

1. ICAO four-letter codes of the departure airport, destination airport and alternate airport.
2. Planned cruise flight level. Flight level allocation is based on the regulations of the civil aviation authority of the locality where the departure airport is located. When departing from airports in mainland China, the RVSM flight level allocation rules implemented by CAAC must be followed.
3. Planned route. The planned route must not contain airport ICAO codes, "SID", "STAR" or procedure names, and must not contain the "DCT" code except for direct routes without route designators designated by the authority or between two points noted as direct in the AIP. For local flights, the planned route shall be entered as "LOCAL".
4. ICAO aircraft type designator.
5. During both activities and routine flights, if there is a controller online in the flight airspace, flight using NAIP non-public route data is prohibited without the controller's permission. During an activity, use of the published activity routes is required unless there are special circumstances, for which a special request may be made to the controller on duty.

**Article 9**　Aircraft pilots shall closely monitor ATC availability during flight. The online flight map can be used to check whether controllers are online in the current flight airspace. Within controlled airspace, the requirements of Article 11 shall be followed.

**Article 10**　Aircraft pilots shall set the transponder to Mode C from entering the departure runway until vacating the destination landing runway, and shall set the transponder to Mode S at all other times. IDENT mode shall be used only when air traffic control requires transponder identification.

**Article 11**　Within controlled airspace, aircraft pilots shall tune the radio communication equipment to the appropriate air traffic service frequency and comply with ATC instructions. Except for avoiding TCAS RA alerts and terrain warnings, unauthorized maneuvering is strictly prohibited.

**Article 12**　After connecting to the server, aircraft pilots shall not violate the civil aviation laws and regulations applicable to the location of the aircraft they are controlling, nor shall they enter published flight restricted areas.

**Article 13**　After connecting to the server, aircraft pilots shall not take off from or land at an airport when the aircraft does not meet the capacity of that airport.

**Article 14**　After connecting to the server, aircraft pilots are strictly prohibited from using time-compression or stand-selection features, and from pausing within activity areas or in the air. The use of the replay function while connected is strictly prohibited. When changing stands, the pilot must first disconnect from the server, change the stand, and then reconnect.

**Article 15**　After connecting to the server, aircraft pilots are strictly prohibited from deliberately colliding with aircraft controlled by other pilots or intentionally disrupting server order.

### Airport Use Rules

**Article 16**　After connecting to the server, when pushing back, taxiing or driving on the ground in airspace not provided with air traffic control services, aircraft pilots shall be aware of the activities of surrounding pilots and yield to each other. When taxiing, if an aircraft pilot is sighted approaching from the left side of the direction of travel, the pilot shall stop taxiing and wait until that pilot has passed before continuing ("left-window yield principle"). In airspace provided with air traffic control services, ATC instructions shall be followed, and the "left-window yield principle" shall also be observed.

**Article 17**　After connecting to the server, in airspace provided with air traffic control services, entering or crossing a runway requires tower controller clearance and confirmation that no other pilot is currently using the runway.

**Article 18**　After connecting to the server, aircraft pilots shall not occupy runways or taxiways for extended periods, and shall use runways and taxiways in accordance with the airport usage instructions.

**Article 19**　After connecting to the server, aircraft pilots shall join routes or instrument approach procedures in accordance with the arrival and departure procedures published in the AIP.

**Article 20**　When two aircraft are making opposing approaches to the same runway, the pilot whose direction of operation matches the real-world direction of operation has priority; if the real-world direction of operation cannot be determined, the pilot on the upwind side has priority. The pilot without priority shall immediately maneuver the aircraft away from the runway extended centerline and the missed approach area, and resume the approach only after the other aircraft has landed and vacated.

**Article 21**　Where the scenery position results in occupying another pilot's runway, the aircraft pilot shall contact the pilot in the runway, temporarily disconnect, and reconnect only after the pilot on the runway has become airborne.

### Route Use Rules

**Article 22**　After connecting to the server, aircraft pilots shall fly according to the route entered in the flight plan and shall not deviate from the route without authorization, except for weather deviations and emergencies. The deviation range shall not exceed twenty nautical miles on either side of the planned route centerline or airway.

**Article 23**　After connecting to the server, aircraft pilots are strictly prohibited from flying in the reverse direction on one-way airways or routes.

**Article 24**　After connecting to the server, during the cruise phase, aircraft pilots shall use the correct flight level in accordance with the flight level allocation prescribed by the local authority. The flight level in the flight plan shall be entered in accordance with the flight level allocation prescribed by the departure authority.

**Article 25**　After connecting to the server, if changing flight levels, aircraft pilots shall notify other potentially affected pilots of their intention, reach an agreement, and then change, so as to minimize the impact on other pilots.

### Online Flight Activity Rules

**Article 26**　During the activity period, all aircraft pilots within the activity airspace must have two-way voice communication capability and comply with controller instructions. English text-based ATC services are also provided for crews who are unable to use voice communication. Acting on one's own initiative or flying AFK is strictly prohibited. Pilots intending to participate in an activity must apply for a clearance at the corresponding position before participating, and must not pass through the activity airspace without authorization.

**Article 27**　Aircraft pilots shall use currently valid aeronautical information and navigation data during online flight activities. During an activity, aircraft pilots may not request radar service from controllers on the grounds that they are unable to comply with controller instructions.

**Article 28**　During an activity, it is not advisable for aircraft pilots to fly activity routes after the clearance release has ended. If an aircraft pilot decides to continue flight, they must contact the relevant controller and act in accordance with the instructions given.

**Article 29**　During an activity, aircraft pilots flying through the activity airspace must contact the appropriate controller before entering the boundary of the activity airspace, report their position, and comply with instructions.

## Chapter III — Violation Handling Methods

**Article 30**　Aircraft pilots who violate the online flight network rules will receive a platform warning. If the violation is not corrected after the warning, they will be removed from the server and may not reconnect within one day.

**Article 31**　During an activity, aircraft pilots who violate the online flight network rules or refuse to obey controller instructions, thereby affecting the normal and smooth conduct of the activity, will be immediately removed from the server and publicized on the forum, and may be prohibited from connecting to the server for one week or more depending on the circumstances.

**Article 32**　Where an aircraft pilot repeatedly or deliberately violates the online flight network rules, the platform reserves the right to prohibit the pilot from connecting to the server for one month or more, and to publicize this in the online flight group chat.

**Article 33**　When removing an aircraft pilot from the server or prohibiting connection, the platform administrator shall retain the following data for inspection:

1. Outside the activity period, retain radar DP screenshots or recordings proving that the pilot violated these rules, together with conversation screenshots or recordings showing that at least three text warnings were issued to the pilot, with at least one minute between each warning.
2. During the activity, retain radar DP screenshots or recordings proving that the pilot violated these rules, together with conversation screenshots or recordings showing that at least one text warning was issued to the pilot. Where the pilot seriously disrupted the order of the activity and prevented it from proceeding normally, retain radar DP screenshots or recordings proving that the pilot violated these rules.
3. Where the conduct described in Article 33 is present, retain screenshots proving that the pilot repeatedly or deliberately violated the online flight network rules.
4. Where the conduct described in Article 8 (1), (2) or (3) is present, retain screenshots proving that the pilot violated these rules. A pilot in violation for the first time will receive a platform warning; if the violation is not corrected after the warning, the pilot will be removed from the server and may not reconnect within one day.
5. Aircraft pilots must strictly comply with these rules when conducting online flight. Violating any provision of these rules may result in serious adverse consequences. XFLYSIM Air Traffic Control Center holds final authority in determining penalties for aircraft pilots.

## Chapter IV — Supplementary Provisions

**Article 34**　These rules take effect at 00:00 Beijing Time on September 26, 2026.

**Article 35**　Personal accounts shall not be lent to or used by others. A verbal warning shall be issued on first discovery; repeat violations result in a seven-day grounding; persistent offenders shall be permanently grounded.

**Article 36**　When flying online without a controller, please use the public frequency 122.800. When a controller comes online, they will make a text call on the public channel (Please contact me on XXX.XX). At that time, the crew shall tune the aircraft's communication panel to the controller's frequency and establish contact. Text-based crews only need to communicate directly via text in the game; voice crews need to connect the XVoice voice software.

**Article 37**　Online flight is a shared environment. Please use civil language and communicate in harmony. Abusing crew members or controllers is prohibited; any such conduct results in immediate permanent grounding.

**Article 38**　The use of codes 7500 and 7600 is prohibited on online flight. In the event of a genuine emergency, 7700 may be squawked; emergency codes must not be squawked under normal circumstances. Callsigns of aircraft involved in real civil aviation accidents must not be used, out of reverence and respect; violations result in direct account ban.

**Article 39**　Platform vulnerabilities must be reported promptly. Any illegal or improper operation carried out by exploiting a platform vulnerability (BUG) shall be the sole responsibility of the user, and the platform reserves the right to impose penalties or ban the account depending on the circumstances.

**Article 40**　For matters not covered by these rules, the final right of interpretation belongs to the XFLYSIM online flight platform.
